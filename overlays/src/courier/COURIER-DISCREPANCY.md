# The Courier Bag — category labelling discrepancy

**Pattern TCB · prepared 7 October 2026**

This is a record of a conflict between the three Courier Bag documents, with
the evidence for each claim, so it can be checked rather than taken on trust.

---

## The short version

There are **two different category systems** in circulation. They use the same
eight letters, A–H, and they mean **different things by them**.

- The **booklet index** (brief for Gio) and the **tutorial itself** use one system.
  They agree with each other completely.
- The **colour-coded A0** uses a different one.

The clearest single example: the **utility organiser**. The tutorial calls it
**A** and builds it first. The colour-coded A0 marks those same pieces **F**.

A maker who sorts their cut pieces using the colour-coded A0 will build eight
piles that do not match the eight chapters of the tutorial. The first thing
they are asked to do on camera — *"put aside everything marked A"* — will hand
them the wrong pieces.

Separately, the **final A0 pattern carries no category markers at all** —
neither letters nor numbers.

## The three documents

| Document | What it is | Category system |
|---|---|---|
| `ROUGHCUT - THE COURRIER BAG - A0 PATTERN.pdf` | The final pattern, 3 × A0 sheets | **None** — TCB codes only |
| `COLOUR_CODED_A0.pdf` | The same 3 sheets with coloured category chips | A–H, **system 2** |
| `COURIER-BAG-A0-brief-for-Gio.pdf` | The booklet index, 4 × A4 | A–H, **system 1** |
| `for_transcripts_2.txt` | Tutorial transcript, 1,399 lines | A–H, **system 1** |

## The two systems, side by side

| Letter | Booklet index + tutorial (system 1) | Colour-coded A0 legend (system 2) |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Utility organiser | Body shell |
| **B** | Side panels & pockets | Body lining |
| **C** | Back, flap, closure & handle | Side pockets |
| **D** | Bottom | Exterior zip pockets |
| **E** | Laptop sleeve | Laptop sleeve |
| **F** | Lining | Utility organiser |
| **G** | Strap & shoulder pad | Strap & shoulder pad |
| **H** | Assembly & binding | Closure & straps |

Only **E** and **G** mean the same thing in both. The other six do not.

System 2's legend is printed along the bottom edge of all three colour-coded
sheets.

## Evidence that the tutorial uses system 1

These are verbatim from the transcript. Line numbers are given so they can be
found.

**The system is letters, A to H** (line 112):

> "by the end of working through **a all the way to h** you should be able to
> have all of the different patterns sewn together ready to put the bag
> together at the end"

**A is the utility organiser** (lines 113–118) — system 2 calls this F:

> "so this is **category a** and this is the bag out of front and then all of
> the panels within this are to create the **utility pocket** […] this is
> probably the most complex part of the pattern so I would very much recommend
> doing this first"

**B is the side panels** (lines 131–148) — system 2 calls this "body lining":

> "then we're going to move on to **B** […] all of these panels right here
> create **the side panels and the pockets** of your bag"

**C is the back, flap, closure and handle** (lines 148–153) — system 2 calls
this "side pockets":

> "next we're going to focus on these patterns right here which they will be
> **C** for you and that is **the back the flap the closure and the handle**"

**D is the bottom** (lines 157–161) — system 2 calls this "exterior zip
pockets":

> "the next panel pieces which are categorized together is your **D** […] and
> that is **the bag bottom the bottom reinforcement strap and the bottom corner
> reinforcement** […] that is very simple just three pattern pieces"

**E is the laptop sleeve** — both systems agree:

> "these panels say **E** for me they will say E for you as well"

**F is the lining** (lines 170–171) — system 2 calls this "utility organiser":

> "the next ones we're going to be focusing on is your **F** so **this is F in
> letters** and this is **all of the lining of the bag**"

**G is the strap** — both systems agree:

> "**the final letter is G** so mine are labeled G and yours will be too this
> essentially is the bag strap the adjustable bag strap"

**H is assembly and binding** (lines 183–185) — system 2 calls this "closure &
straps":

> "then we pretty much just do all the binding which in your booklet will say
> **H** so **assembly and binding is the letter H**"

**And again later in the build** (lines 755–758), confirming A and B in use:

> "that is everything of **category A**. So all of your panel pieces that have
> A on them. So we're now going to focus in on these panels right here […]
> Yours will have **B** on it. […] So we just tackled A, which was **the
> utility pocket, the front panel, the bottom Cobra clip**. Now we're going to
> focus in on B. So for B, you should have your **bag and lining side** and you
> should have your **side corner reinforcement** […] and your **bag side buckle
> strap**."

Every one of these matches the booklet index group for group, including the
contents of each group. The tutorial and the index are the same document in two
forms.

## Four codes where the documents disagree about whether a piece exists

The booklet index lists these under *"Four codes with no piece"* and asks for a
decision. On the colour-coded A0 **they are drawn as real pieces**:

| Code | On the colour-coded A0 | In the booklet index |
|---|---|---|
| **TCB-034** | Exists — "Front Internal Pocket, cut × 1", sheet 3 | *"Almost certainly now split into those two, so retire 034"* |
| **TCB-036** ✔ | Exists — "Utility Internal Pocket Top Pocket, cut × 1", sheet 2 | *"either one of these is the closure-tab strip described above under a new number, or they are genuinely retired"* |
| **TCB-037** ✔ | Exists — "Utility Internal Pocket Top Panel, cut × 1", sheet 2 | as above |
| **TCB-007** | Exists — "Front Zipper Pocket, cut × 1", sheet 1 | *"It only has a home if the plain front alternative to the organiser is still in the pattern. If it is, keep it […] If not, retire it"* |

This suggests the colour-coded A0 was built from a **pattern state before those
questions were settled**, which would also explain the different grouping.

## The final A0 has no category markers

All three sheets of `ROUGHCUT - THE COURRIER BAG - A0 PATTERN.pdf` carry the
piece name, cut count and TCB code — but **no category letter and no number**
anywhere, and no legend strip along the bottom.

This matters because the tutorial tells the viewer the markers will already be
there (lines 106–110):

> "right next to that coding you should have a letter — I have hand drawn some
> of the letters on my pattern pieces right here but you will have already
> pre-sort of written before printing, **you should have these letters already
> on the co-existing patterns**"

So whichever system is settled on, the markers need to be **on the sheet the
customer actually downloads**, not only in the booklet.

---

## What I would check

1. **Decide which system is correct.** The tutorial is already filmed, so the
   audio is fixed. That is a strong argument for the booklet index's system
   being the one to keep, and the colour-coded A0 being the one to re-export.
2. **Re-export the colour-coded A0 from the settled grouping.** It is currently
   the most dangerous document in the folder, because it looks authoritative,
   it is in colour, and it is the one a maker will naturally sort from.
3. **Settle TCB-007, 034, 036 and 037** — each is either a real piece or
   retired, and the two documents currently answer differently.
4. **Get the category markers onto the final A0** so the sheet matches what the
   tutorial says the viewer will be looking at.
5. **Check the booklet, the index map and the construction manual** against
   whichever system wins — the brief says all three are built off these codes.

## How this was checked

- The transcript was read in full and every category announcement located by
  line number; the quotes above are verbatim, including the surrounding
  sentences that identify each group's contents.
- The colour-coded A0 was rendered at 100 dpi for the overview and at 220 dpi
  for the items marked ✔, where the TCB code and the category chip were read
  together on the same piece.
- The booklet index groups were taken from page 2 of the brief for Gio, and the
  open-code questions from page 3.
- Group contents were matched both ways: by piece code, and by the plain-English
  description of each group in the tutorial against the description in the index.

The one thing I could not verify is the live site — `roughcutpatterns.com` is
not reachable from the machine this was prepared on, so if a newer index has
been published there it was not part of this comparison.
